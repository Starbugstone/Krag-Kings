# Outrageous machinery, heavy bionics and weapons

Draft 0.5 · 4 October 2026 · [Pack overview](00-overview-and-campaign.md)

**Established direction from the latest feedback:** the game needs a much wilder mechanical personality. Oversized steampunk bionic claws, replacement metal jaws, vehicle wrecking balls and missile launchers belong in the design. The specific equipment names, rules and numbers below are draft proposals.

This is a tonal expansion of the original game, not a switch to flat cartoon art. Keep the textured, semi-realistic rendering and make the objects, proportions, animation and crew behaviour delightfully excessive. A heavy claw should read as a heavy claw from the tactical camera, not as an ordinary prosthetic with a small bonus.

## 1. The machinery should create the joke

Give equipment a memorable action and an obvious silhouette: a claw almost as large as its owner's torso, a powered metal jaw with uneven iron teeth, a demolition ball hanging off a truck that looks much too proud of itself, or four enormous mismatched missiles bolted above a tiny control seat.

Steampunk elements can include exposed pistons, large gears, brass pressure dials, copper lines, flywheels, riveted housings and theatrical steam vents. Combine them with the desert scrapyard identity. Do not cover every object in tiny gears that vanish at gameplay distance.

The intended appeal of the earlier inspiration is reckless invention, swagger, improvised excess and physical comedy. Express that through Krags, Nibs and their own visual language, voices and equipment.

Krags' established biology and temperament reinforce this: they feel little pain, have tough skin, are fearless and enjoy fighting. Bigger guns are objects of pride as well as useful equipment. The clan boss is physically bigger than the other Krags and should be an obvious candidate for the most excessive portable gun. Give the crew eager reactions to the machinery rather than making them constantly terrified of using it. The trait, support and boss rules live in [02-characters-jetpacks-and-bionics.md](02-characters-jetpacks-and-bionics.md); their precise armour/health values remain test settings.

### Reliable enough to plan around

Ordinary use should follow understandable rules. The humour comes from awkward reach, huge impact, exposed operators, scarce ammunition, a wide swing and the decision to use a ridiculous tool in a cramped place.

Optional overcharging can add explicit failure odds later. Do not make every attack a concealed chance to explode or injure its owner. A successful plan must still feel like the player's success.

## 2. Heavy bionics

### Who can carry the machinery

Krags willingly accept heavy anatomical replacements, subject to sockets and their implant strain allowance. Nibs' fragile bodies cannot sustain Krag heavy parts and support only limited lightweight enhancements. The crusher claw and iron jaw below are Krag-only; a small strain value never overrides a species restriction. Mounted giant weapons and fitted jetpacks remain available to Nibs because their external supports are checked separately. Fitting a new limb has real campaign downtime even for a fearless Krag. [10-experience-wounds-and-recovery.md](10-experience-wounds-and-recovery.md) defines the item compatibility, XP/perk, wound and recovery rules.

### Crusher claw

**Visual:** an anatomical arm replacement ending in a gigantic three-finger industrial pincer. A reinforced elbow/shoulder mount supports brass cylinders, oversized knuckles, copper lines and a conspicuous pressure gauge. The claw remains on the same anatomical side in every view and animation.

**Role:** close-range boarding brute and component breaker. It can crush an exposed wheel or mount, attack an adjacent enemy, and provide a strong handhold. Tearing armour open, lifting an opponent and throwing them off a roof are later extensions, not automatic extra effects on every hit.

**First POC rule:** Crusher strike costs 1 AP, deals 5 damage and ignores 1 point of armour, using the usual melee hit check. It requires a reachable adjacent ground target or connected vehicle position; maximum physical reach is initially 1.8 m. Component attacks require an actually exposed, reachable component. The claw's grip benefit is +10 percentage points on risky boarding checks, capped as usual; it does not stack with a second grip bonus on the same arm.

The claw is a perk-granting bionic available through injury treatment or elective replacement, subject to the same strain and downtime. It occupies its arm socket and replaces that hand. It cannot operate small controls or support a two-handed rifle in the first rules. The other hand may carry a compact one-handed firearm using the existing basic gun profile for the POC. The animation should have a satisfying mechanical wind-up without seizing the camera.

Later **Grab and wrench** could establish a contested hold before a pull, throw or sabotage action. That needs a real held-state rule and counterplay; do not smuggle a free grapple into the first strike implementation.

### Iron jaw

**Visual:** a mechanical lower jaw integrated into the mouth and cheek hinges, with large blunt iron teeth and expressive pistons. It is a replacement jaw, not simply an armoured mask. Clacking teeth and badly muffled radio replies give it personality.

**Role:** an additional way to fight while the hands are busy, such as clinging to an external rail. Its value is action access and character identity, not a universal damage multiplier.

**First POC rule:** Bite costs 1 AP and uses the basic melee profile: 3 damage and the ordinary species melee hit chance. It can target an adjacent exposed occupant while the attacker is in a valid supported/hanging node, even if their hands are occupied. It does not add a free second attack after a claw strike, work through a closed cab, or replace a grip requirement. A character still needs a valid way to remain attached while biting.

The jaw is now an explicit equipment/anatomy socket. More elaborate effects such as biting through a restraint or chewing exposed machinery can follow once their interactions exist.

### Other bionic candidates

| Candidate | Distinct use | Limiting choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Piston fist | One forceful shove or breach | Short reach and a telegraphed action |
| Spring legs | A dramatic leap without sustained flight | Landing space and poorer stability |
| Boiler chest | Fuel an overdrive ability | Visible heat/pressure management |
| Tool carousel arm | Swap repair/sabotage heads | Gives up the heavy combat hand |
| Harpoon hand | Create a boarding tether | Occupies the hand and exposes the tether |
| Stomp boot | A local knockdown or soft-cover smash | Grounded, adjacent targets only |

These are a future catalogue, not six extra systems required for the basic POC.

## 3. Vehicle-mounted mayhem

Special mounts use the same stations, component damage, operator actions and attachment metadata as ordinary guns. A bigger weapon does not generate a new activation or a free attack after movement.

The standard truck, wrecking rig and missile rig are fitting variants of one chassis. The first build has one main heavy-mount slot, so the gun, crane and missile rack are alternatives. Combining all three on a monster chassis belongs to later roster design.

Alongside the mounted weapons, give Gorr a portable oversized heavy gun with its own 4-damage/60%-base profile and support requirement. Krag strength, or a suitable mount/brace for a smaller user, supplies that support. One-handed boss variants need a visible built-in brace and a declared one-hand requirement, making them compatible with a claw arm without pretending that all two-handed guns work that way.

### The Argument: wrecking-ball rig

**Visual:** a heavily braced crane at the rear of the truck, a visibly connected chain and an enormous riveted iron ball with blunt studs. A winch, large flywheel, pressure dial, counterweight and travel cradle make its state readable. The ball must be recognisable even in a wide tactical view.

**Role:** threaten a flank, breach soft barricades and force enemies to respect nearby space. The driver must create a useful angle; the operator decides when to swing.

**First POC rule:** a staffed mount can swing once per round for 1 operator AP while the truck is Stopped or Slow. The planned sweep occupies an authored outboard sector, initially up to 60 degrees around the right/rear working area, with the ball centre travelling about 3.5 m from its pivot. The initial ball collider radius is 0.65 m. These are gameplay values to fit the greybox, not measurements from the concept image.

Resolve the whole swept ball volume against terrain, components and characters in contact order. The first physical contact receives 8 nominal damage before armour and stops the swing in the simple POC. A designated soft barricade is broken by that hit. Follow-through and several objects in one sweep can be added later. An aware character can use their available evasion reaction when a legal escape point exists.

Friendly crew can be struck if they occupy the sweep. Show that risk before confirmation. A protected occupant is not separately hit through an intact host collider. The valid operating arc excludes the host chassis and its occupied operator position; it cannot phase through the truck's cab to reach an enemy.

The ball is stowed before ordinary travel and after the attack sequence, using a validated recovery path. Recovery animation causes no bonus damage. If geometry would prevent safe return to the cradle, reject that planned swing in the POC. Later persistent deployed/stuck states may be interesting, but must be deliberately implemented.

**Fit trade-off:** the crane/cradle uses the truck's whole transport pool, leaving no cargo or ordinary passenger load. The driver and dedicated operator remain. Maximum speed is Fast. The working-side external rail is unavailable when operating the mount. This makes it a specialised fighting rig that still needs a hauler for salvage.

### Bad Idea: missile rack

**Visual:** four absurdly large mismatched scrap missiles on a tilting rack, with chunky fins, uneven casings, crooked painted faces, a crank and a tiny operator perch. The sheet shows four sockets in a 2 × 2 arrangement. External art communicates invented game machinery; the model does not need real weapon internals.

**Role:** a limited-ammunition area threat. It punishes clustered exposed units, denies comfortable cover and makes firing near a boarding party a serious decision.

The first gameplay version is an unguided rocket/missile rack. Guided, homing or target-seeking variants are separate later abilities rather than implied by the word missile.

**Initial rule for the follow-on weapon milestone:** 4 missiles loaded, 2 fired per salvo, 1 operator AP, at most 1 salvo per round, no in-battle reload. Fire while Stopped or Slow, at an aimed point 8–35 m away. Each projectile scatters within a displayed 2 m disk using the authoritative seeded RNG.

Preview the full possible affected area: scatter radius plus blast radius, along with the centre of aim and visible obstructions. Each resolved impact has a 2.5 m blast radius, dealing 6 damage within 1 m and 3 beyond that within the radius, before armour. Resolve terrain blocking between the actual impact and each target. A solid wall stops blast damage behind it in this initial model.

The projectile path collides with the first obstruction before its sampled endpoint. Friendly fire is real and shown in the preview. One missile may damage a character once and at most one exposed component on each vehicle; choose the nearest exposed component, falling back to the frame, rather than multiplying damage across every collider on one rig. A second missile can hit it again. Protected occupants are only separately exposed when a declared damage/access state permits it.

Launching reserves/spends the two missiles once. Cancelled previews consume nothing. Resolve the two launched projectiles in stable order, updating destroyed cover between impacts. Do not automatically turn a destroyed rack into a second explosion in the initial version; ammunition cook-off is a separate future rule.

**Fit trade-off:** the rack leaves only 2 of the truck's 8 transport load, blocks the gun slot and most deck boarding space, and caps speed at Fast. It cannot carry the 4-load mission engine. Keep a cargo-capable truck in the encounter.

### Tin Can mounts and advanced Nib weapons

Tin Cans reuse operator actions, component damage and module profiles. Their Nib pilot is supported by the chassis rather than heavy body implants; the vehicle cannot board other vehicles. Wheeled/legged and gun/claw options, incoming hatch access and a first advanced carbine are specified in [12-tin-cans-and-nib-technology.md](12-tin-cans-and-nib-technology.md). These are follow-on fixtures, not additional prerequisites for the first truck movement test.

## 4. More toys for the longer-term catalogue

| Item | Tactical idea | Main dependency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Harpoon winch | Catch a rig or assist a boarding approach | Tethers, tension limits and release rules |
| Springboard launcher | Deliver a willing boarder onto another vehicle | Existing landing/boarding validation plus visible landing risk |
| Rotating scrap saw | Threaten a narrow vehicle flank | Swept contact and exposed operator rules |
| Pneumatic boxing bumper | Push a light target without a full-speed ram | Bounded displacement and collision validation |
| Scrap lobber | Lob a clumsy area projectile over cover | Arcing projectile and blast-cover rules |
| Steam vent | Force nearby external riders to reposition | Short-range hazard, exposure and cooldown |
| Boost boiler | Trade engine condition for a spectacular burst | Overdrive resource and declared failure outcomes |

Each should have one distinctive action before adding several upgrade tiers. Avoid a catalogue of different-looking items that all merely increase damage by 5%.

## 5. Make the POC demonstrate the tone

The first integrated encounter includes Brok with the crusher claw and iron jaw, Gorr as the larger clan boss at the friendly truck's gun station with a portable heavy-gun option, and a hostile truck fitted as The Argument. Fiz drives the friendly truck and repairs it; that truck remains the salvage carrier. The enemy operator uses the crane station instead of an additional gun; the crew/vehicle count stays small. Gorr may use the existing claw/jaw fittings as an optional loadout, as shown in the boss concept, without requiring a new ability system.

Build a safe test lane for the swing, with a breachable barrier, one exposed target and a marked friendly-risk position. Add the missile rack as the next content milestone using a separate fixture or alternate encounter setup, after sweep and exposure rules work. It must retain a standard cargo-capable carrier so the objective remains possible.

Greybox claws, a sphere on an animated boom, a hinged jaw and simple projectile meshes are enough to test these ideas. Full chained rigid-body simulation, deep heat/pressure mechanics and bespoke cinematics are unnecessary for the first proof.

## 6. Presentation and acceptance

Show the claw's actual reach, the ball's full sweep and the missile's possible blast envelope. Distinguish target precision from outcome uncertainty. The game should never label a whole wrecking-ball sweep harmless because its final point misses the friendly Nib standing halfway along it.

Good feedback includes a pressure needle jumping, a claw snapping shut, the chassis rocking under a swing, an exaggerated jaw clack and rockets wobbling as they depart. These are bounded animations driven by resolved events, not sources of additional damage or action consumption.

The design succeeds when a player remembers a specific manoeuvre: biting an enemy while clinging to the rail, crushing a turret, forcing a wrecker to turn the wrong way, or holding a missile salvo because their own boarder is too close. These should be ordinary possibilities of the rules, not rare scripted jokes.
