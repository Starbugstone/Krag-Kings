# A raid at Rusthook — gameplay design and narrated story

Draft 0.5 · 4 October 2026 · [Pack overview](00-overview-and-campaign.md)

**User clarification — 5 October 2026:** this narrative is for understanding world, lore and tone, **not the final scenario**. Its dialogue, injuries, outcome and sequence are not mandatory POC events. The depot-salvage gameplay objective was selected separately in the current planning conversation.

This is an illustrative playthrough of the current design: preparation, an engine-recovery raid, extraction, experience, wounds and Brok's leg replacement. Outcomes and dialogue are examples rather than scripted events. In particular, Brok is not guaranteed to suffer this injury in every Rusthook mission.

The full game includes a fort and expeditions. The POC uses loadout, battle and debrief screens, followed by a small recovery/perk demonstration. Exact XP, wound, species compatibility and downtime rules live in [10-experience-wounds-and-recovery.md](10-experience-wounds-and-recovery.md). This example begins with 0 XP on each crew member.

## 1. Preparing the gang

**Gameplay design.** A rival gang has uncovered a valuable engine at Rusthook depot. Release it from its cradle, load it onto a compatible truck, and extract. The expedition needs fighting power, objective access and cargo capacity.

| Crew | Starting role | Distinct contribution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Gorr | Larger Krag clan boss, truck gunner | Heavy firepower, tough skin, physically larger body and portable heavy gun |
| Fiz | Nib truck driver/mechanic | Movement, repair and eventual extraction compete for his actions |
| Brok | Regular Krag sidecar passenger/boarder | Crusher claw, strong grip and iron-jaw attack |
| Tikk | Nib bike rider, fitted jetpack | Flanking transport, vertical access and objective interaction |

You retain the ordinary cargo-capable truck. A wrecking-ball fitting would fill its transport space. The bike carries Brok in its sidecar. Gorr cannot substitute into that sidecar: his body and passenger load exceed its fit.

**Narrated story.**

Gorr stands beside the truck while Fiz tightens the gun mounting. Even among Krags, the boss looks like someone assembled him using parts from a larger model.

“Bigger barrel,” says Gorr.

“Then it hits the cab when you turn it.”

Gorr considers this.

“Smaller cab.”

Brok tests his claw on a length of scrap pipe. Three steel fingers close, the pressure needle twitches, and the pipe develops an entirely new shape. Tikk moves the bike a little farther away before starting it.

## 2. Entering the depot

**Gameplay design.** The tactical camera shows a broad vehicle lane, scrap cover, ruins and a gantry. This illustrative layout places the release console on the gantry, reachable by stairs or jetpack; the final scene still needs measured route testing.

The enemy has a wrecking-ball truck with driver and crane operator, plus a guard on foot. Its authored working arc threatens part of the yard, not the entire map. Solid terrain and approach direction offer counterplay.

Sides alternate activation groups. Each character receives two actions. Fiz spends his own actions driving; Gorr spends his own firing. The truck gets at most one normal move per round. The account below compresses several activations and enemy responses rather than implying that the entire gang performs one uninterrupted combo.

**Narrated story.**

Fiz steers behind a concrete obstruction as the enemy crane winds up. Its ball smashes a light scrap screen farther across the yard. Corrugated metal folds around it with a noise like a workshop falling downstairs.

Gorr watches the chain reel back.

“We're taking that.”

“We came for an engine.”

“We're taking two things.”

Fiz adjusts the route. Gorr adjusts the gun. For once, the two adjustments point in approximately the same direction.

## 3. Brok makes an introduction

**Gameplay design.** Tikk brings the bike around the wrecker's non-working flank. The preview identifies a usable handhold, the boarding cost and any hazardous transition check. Brok spends one action boarding and retains one.

In this example, an exposed occupant is within reach of Brok's supported hanging node. His hands are busy keeping him attached, so the iron jaw provides an attack he can actually perform there. Bite spends the remaining action. It wounds the operator rather than granting an automatic kill. Brok cannot then gain a fresh independent activation this round.

**Narrated story.**

Brok catches the rail with his claw. His boots scrape the truck's side as the bike continues past.

The operator leans over to dislodge him.

Brok's jaw opens with a hydraulic hiss.

There is a metallic clack, a shout, and a considerable disagreement over ownership of the operator's sleeve.

“Brok's aboard,” Tikk reports.

Gorr sounds pleased. “He introducing himself?”

**Consequence.** Contact is not capture. The driver still controls the truck. Brok must survive, move through valid connected positions and deal with reachable occupants or components.

## 4. Tikk takes the high route

**Gameplay design.** Tikk parks the bike safely. On an activation with both actions available, dismounting costs one and the jump costs the other, along with one of two jetpack charges. The chosen gantry landing is within the pack's range and has a valid footprint.

The console interaction waits for a later activation. Landing grants neither a free shot nor a free objective action. Tikk remains exposed during flight, and the stairs remain a useful route for a crew without packs.

**Narrated story.**

The jetpack coughs, flashes, and throws Tikk over the scrap wall. He lands beside the console with his ears flattened beneath his goggles.

Below, Brok is still attached to the enemy truck.

“Found the release!”

“Release what?” Gorr asks.

Fiz closes his eyes for a moment. “The reason we're here.”

## 5. Toughness has limits

**Gameplay design.** An ordinary three-damage physical shot hits Gorr without a graze. Natural armour reduces it to two. He loses health but suffers no pain-only action penalty and does not panic.

His bulk still affects cover. If he leaves the gun station to use his portable weapon, a low barrier that hides Tikk may leave much of Gorr exposed. He needs a valid exit, a route that fits his body and ordinary AP for each action.

Damage to the friendly truck's engine forces Fiz to stop progressing toward the loading point. Over subsequent activations he reaches the repair access and works on the component. Driving and repairs are not free simultaneous jobs.

After several exchanges aboard the wrecker, Brok reaches and finishes disabling its already-damaged crane mount. During the struggle he suffers a structural injury to his left leg. In this run, its effects are ordinary foot movement reduced from 10 m to 6 m, Sprint unavailable, and -15 percentage points to risky boarding checks.

**Narrated story.**

Gorr examines the mark across his shoulder, then looks towards the guard.

“That one's awake.”

He sounds delighted.

Fiz is less delighted about the engine. “We need this truck to leave.”

“It's still making noise.”

“So are you. Keep the gun occupied.”

Across the yard, Brok's claw closes around the battered crane mechanism. Something gives. The operator's controls suddenly become an elaborate way of doing nothing.

Brok drops his weight onto his left leg and it fails to hold him. He catches the rail before he falls.

“Leg's gone stupid.”

“Does it hurt?” Tikk asks.

“No. Just keeps pointing wrong.”

**Consequence.** Low pain changes Brok's reaction, not his anatomy. His injury matters immediately and will survive the debrief. The gang needs to get him home.

## 6. Recovering the prize

**Gameplay design.** Tikk releases the engine. Fiz gets the repaired carrier into position and stops it. A character uses the loading-cradle access to secure the released engine into the compatible truck.

The standard truck has eight transport load, with its dedicated driver and gunner accounted for separately. The engine uses four; Brok as an ordinary passenger uses two; Tikk would use one. That totals seven. Stowing the bike as well would require nine, so everything cannot go aboard together.

You choose to ride the bike out. In this example it was parked within a legal return jump of the gantry. Tikk spends his final charge and one action returning to clear ground, then an action mounting. Bike movement must wait for the next legal activation. Brok uses a safe supported route onto the stopped truck, subject to his wound and the actual access geometry.

**Narrated story.**

The engine settles into its restraints. Brok drags himself into the passenger position and gives his left knee a reproachful look.

Across the yard, the enemy driver revs his wrecker. Its weapon is finished, but its bumper remains an argument.

Gorr looks towards it.

Fiz points at the engine. “Paid.”

Then at Brok's leg. “Expensive.”

Then towards the exit. “Home.”

Gorr gives the rival driver an offended glare, as though leaving an unfinished fight is exceptionally poor hospitality.

**Consequence.** You extract the carrier with its engine and Brok aboard, securing the primary objective. The encounter continues until Tikk also leaves on the bike, then the gang confirms final extraction. You do not need to kill the remaining driver. Staying to capture the wrecker would require clearing its controls, assigning a driver and extracting it; its crane-filled bed would still provide no engine cargo space.

## 7. Experience and damage arrive together

**Gameplay design.** All four returned and contributed: Gorr damaged a hostile, Fiz repaired genuine battle damage and extracted the carrier, Brok boarded, and Tikk released the engine. Each receives 1 participation XP + 2 primary-objective XP + 1 capped contribution XP = **4 XP**. No kills are required for equal credit.

Brok reaches the first threshold and earns a perk point. You choose **Sure Grip**, granting +5 percentage points to legal risky boarding checks. His leg debuff remains. XP and a perk do not cure him, and being wounded does not delete what he learned.

The debrief shows the raid's returns, repairs, wounds, XP breakdown and treatment options. Opening it again cannot repeat the award.

**Narrated story.**

Back at the fort, Brok explains the boarding manoeuvre three times. By the third telling, the truck was moving faster and the operator had acquired several extra friends.

Tikk demonstrates the handhold Brok actually used. Brok tries the improved grip, turns his claw slightly, and nods.

“Do that next time.”

Then he stands, and remembers the leg.

## 8. A new leg is a job, not an instant cure

**Gameplay design.** The gang returned on campaign day 1. You have a compatible replacement and an available Sawbones treatment place. You choose a reinforced piston leg. Starting treatment commits the part once and immediately makes Brok unavailable for deployment.

The proposed treatment takes three in-game days: fitting from day 1 to day 2, then recovery and calibration until day 4. This is campaign time advanced by player choices, not three real days of waiting.

Brok's crusher claw uses three strain, his jaw one and the new leg three: **7/8 Krag implant strain**. His enthusiasm does not waive the time needed to make the attachment functional.

**Narrated story.**

The Sawbones lays out two replacement legs. One looks practical. The other contains a piston large enough to deserve its own warning sign.

Brok points. “That one.”

“Three days.”

“Doesn't hurt.”

“That's not the measurement.”

Brok inspects the new foot. “Can you make it louder?”

The Sawbones takes the foot away before he can help.

## 9. The gang keeps moving while Brok recovers

**Gameplay design.** At day 2 the leg has been fitted, but Brok is still recovering and cannot be selected for deployment. Choosing perks, healing ordinary battle health or moving him to a different vehicle slot cannot bypass this lock.

You can rest at the fort or take another expedition with the other three, where its roster requirements permit that. A one-day expedition advances the treatment by one day when its elapsed time is committed; it does not complete the entire recovery merely because a mission ended. Brok stays at the fort even if he becomes ready while the others are away.

For this narrated branch, you stay, repair equipment and explicitly advance the calendar through days 2 and 3. Each elapsed day checks the campaign event table and stops for decisions. This illustrative branch has no event that changes the departure plan; the actual run can offer a rumour, trader or another eligible event.

**Narrated story.**

On the first recovery day, Brok discovers that a newly fitted leg can kick a crate quite hard and still be unsuitable for walking across a room. The Sawbones confiscates the crate.

Fiz works at the next bench with a compact optical unit and a lightweight replacement grip. Together they would fill a Nib's two-point implant allowance.

Brok nods towards his claw. “Get one of these.”

“That arm weighs like half my body.”

“Good half.”

Fiz taps the truck's heavy-gun mounting. “That carries the weight. I carry the tools.”

**Species consequence.** Nibs can use enormous weapons supported by a vehicle and can wear fitted equipment such as jetpacks. Their bodies cannot take Krag heavy implants. They can have a limited number of lightweight enhancements or species-rated restorative prostheses; both consume their implant allowance. A high level or an empty strain budget does not make a crusher claw compatible.

## 10. Brok returns on day 4

**Gameplay design.** Completing recovery restores the damaged leg's ordinary function. Brok gains Power Step from the implant: 12 m Move and 20 m Sprint, while the leg's -15-point boarding penalty ends. His Sure Grip perk remains, his existing claw and jaw still work, and the new leg changes neither his two-action budget nor his ordinary Krag health template.

His record permanently links the Rusthook injury and its replacement. The visible piston leg replaces the injury presentation. Unrelated wounds would retain their own effects. The reinforced leg both restores function and grants its named movement perk. A healthy Krag could elect the same replacement, with the same strain and recovery requirements.

**Narrated story.**

Brok walks the length of the yard. He turns, walks back, then tests the first step of the truck's boarding rail.

The piston settles with a satisfying clunk.

“Better.”

Fiz watches his footing. “Working.”

“Same thing.”

Across the garage, Gorr has found a sketch of four enormous missiles bolted over a truck.

“Fiz.”

“No.”

“You haven't heard the question.”

“I can see the question.”

## 11. What this example asks the POC to prove

The battle should generate memorable incidents through ordinary rules: a supported bite while boarding, a jetpack route that still costs actions, a fearless Krag with a real structural injury, and extraction that depends on a functioning carrier. The aftermath should retain those consequences and offer understandable choices.

The minimum continuation is a debrief XP award, one perk choice, one wound, one perk-granting replacement, event-aware campaign-day advances and an enforced return-to-duty state. File 11 adds stable casualty recovery, rescue and slower foot-return branches to the connected test. The 3D fort, extra expeditions, surgery animations, larger perk tree and complete economy remain later work. None is required to demonstrate that Brok gained experience, lost availability for a time, and returned with a different body.
