# Tin Cans and advanced Nib weapons

Draft 0.5 · 4 October 2026 · [Pack overview](00-overview-and-campaign.md)

**User direction — 5 October 2026:** a Tin Can must read as a **tall, round tin can or barrel**, refitted from scrap junk, with extensive modular customization and varied options. **Only Nibs can pilot it.** Keep the cylinder recognizable through armor and module changes; a generic box-shaped humanoid mech is not the target. The user confirmed **concept art and integration planning only for this POC**, with no playable Tin Can. New concept exploration is registered in [18](18-strategic-concept-art.md).

**Established:** Nibs can manipulate higher-level weapon technology. Krags favour large, loud guns, heavy mechanical limbs and close combat. Nibs can pilot scrap-built Tin Cans with interchangeable legs/wheels and weapon/claw equipment. **A Tin Can cannot board another vehicle.**

The exact profiles below are proposals for follow-on content after the basic battle. [File 13](13-relay-station-narrated-mission.md) illustrates a possible second mission. The original ten concept sheets did not include a Tin Can or advanced Nib weapon sheet. A new Tin Can exploration is now registered in [file 18](18-strategic-concept-art.md); it is not a completed model or a production-approved module catalogue.

## 1. A vehicle around a fragile pilot

A Tin Can is a separate vehicle entity with a Nib pilot, not a heavy anatomical implant. Its frame carries weapon loads and recoil. It does not consume the pilot's implant strain budget. The pilot's actual bionics retain their usual limits, and entering the cockpit does not temporarily erase an injury or treatment lock.

Nibs retain reasons to dismount: small paths, consoles, salvage handling, repair access and fitted jetpack routes. A Tin Can is conspicuous, bulky and needs a passable vehicle route. Its gun does not operate without a conscious, compatible pilot.

The first proposed chassis has one protected Nib-sized cockpit, zero ordinary passenger/cargo capacity and two arm module sockets. No extra gunner or anonymous crew. Nib-only piloting is now an explicit species requirement, enforced alongside body/gear fit in the rules and save validation. A Krag does not become a permitted pilot by changing the seat or shrinking the model.

## 2. Pilot actions and boarding

The pilot keeps their ordinary two AP and activation identity. Moving costs 1 pilot AP and uses the chassis's one normal movement allowance. A shot or claw attack costs 1 pilot AP. Two arms give equipment options, not free attacks. Entering or leaving a stopped machine costs 1 AP through its hatch with valid ground space. Entering after already acting does not refresh AP or chassis movement.

A Tin Can has no Board command, no powered boarding jump and no ability to occupy another vehicle's rail, deck or station. Walking legs do not change this. A dismounted Nib can board using ordinary character rules if their remaining AP, gear and geometry permit it.

**Proposed incoming boarding rule:** enemies may use one exposed hatch-rail node on the Tin Can, if the ordinary approach is legal. They can attack an exposed pilot only when the hatch/access state actually permits it, or sabotage a reachable component. Contact does not grant cockpit control. An attacker unable to fit the cockpit cannot pilot the machine after clearing it. Incoming boarding is distinct from the Tin Can's forbidden outgoing boarding.

The hatch rail is not a free friendly passenger transport slot. A driver shake-off remains subject to a separately specified manoeuvre; the first follow-on can use ordinary attacks/sabotage to contest the rail. Disabled pilots and frame destruction use [file 11's casualty and escape rules](11-campaign-events-rescue-and-retreat.md).

## 3. First wheeled fitting

| Property | Starting proposal |
| --- | --- |
| Body envelope | About 2.4 m high, 1.8 m wide, 2.2 m long before protruding arms; validate greybox |
| Frame / armour | 16 health / 2 armour |
| Power unit / each wheel / each module | 6 / 3 / 6 health |
| Locomotion | Four wheels; maximum Fast |
| Slow / Fast travel | 0–8 m / 6–16 m |
| Minimum turn radius, Slow / Fast | 2 m / 4 m |
| One disabled wheel | Maximum travel reduced by 40% |
| Two disabled wheels or disabled power unit | No powered movement; pilot may still use working systems or leave |
| Cargo and passenger pool | 0; pilot included in chassis baseline |

Use validated curved movement and the ordinary braking/coasting contract. Arms are included in the swept footprint. No hovering, special pivot teleport or footprint that shrinks to pass a doorway. Frame damage does not secretly injure the pilot through an intact cockpit; the declared frame-destruction event is handled once.

Field repair restores a repairable power unit or module using the existing charge/AP limits. Wheel replacement and repair of a destroyed frame require the fort or a future recovery system. An immobilised machine can be abandoned with a salvage-return marker while its pilot escapes; the game does not assume a towing feature exists.

## 4. Modules and locomotion options

| Fitting | Useful decision | Initial boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Heavy gun arm | Existing mounted-gun profile: 4 damage, 40 m, 75% base hit, 1 AP | Legal arc/LOS; normal pilot AP; one module slot |
| Crusher arm | Existing crusher profile: 5 damage, ignore 1 armour, 1 AP, reachable target within 1.8 m | The whole machine must be in range; does not let it board or grasp a moving rig |
| Wheels | Faster travel on suitable lanes | Turning room, steps and running-gear damage |
| Walking legs — later fitting | Better rubble/low-step access and controlled rotation | Slower travel, real support/clearance, no ladders or boarding; exact locomotion tune deferred |
| Advanced emitter — later module | Precision or a defined power-system interaction | Needs its own complete weapon profile; not a universal disable-anything button |

Both arm sockets may eventually carry weapons, but each action operates one module. The first fixture uses one heavy gun and an empty second socket, reducing art and UI work. Two different modules can be used in one activation if the pilot has two AP and both attacks are legal; movement competes for those same actions. No hidden chassis attack pool.

Changing wheel/leg assemblies and arm modules occurs at the garage between expeditions. Initially use authored loadouts; workshop prices/time and free-form assembly are later progression work. A captured fitting remains equipped until deliberately changed.

## 5. Nib technology affinity

Represent the species direction through starting training and access to advanced modes. Initial Nibs have Advanced Control proficiency. Krags prefer their ordinary loadouts; future training may give a Krag that proficiency without changing body size or implant capacity. This preference must not become a blanket prohibition on all technical weapons.

### Needlebeam carbine — first advanced weapon

| Rule | Prototype value |
| --- | --- |
| Standard pulse | 1 AP, 3 energy damage, armour penetration 1, 30 m range |
| Base hit chance | 80% with Advanced Control; 65% without it, subject to ordinary physical hand/fit requirements |
| Focused pulse | 2 AP, 4 energy damage, armour penetration 2; requires Advanced Control |
| Energy supply | 6 charges per encounter; standard shot 1, focused shot 2; no in-battle recharge |
| Aim / cover / target motion | Existing modifiers and 5–95% cap; focused mode does not provide a free Aim action |
| Armour and graze | Direct energy hits use applicable worn and Krag natural armour; apply penetration normally; character ranged graze still applies |

The POC supply screen starts the encounter with a full cell, just as it supplies jetpack charges. An empty weapon remains empty until the encounter ends; no paid or real-time recharge. A carried sidearm can be selected through a 1 AP weapon-swap action; no free swap-shot when AP is exhausted.

Neither firing mode bypasses line of sight, closed cabs or component exposure. Focused pulse improves the declared penetration, not an assumed ability to shut down an entire truck. Weapon noise and colour are presentation here; stealth/noise propagation and electronic-disruption statuses remain later rules.

Keep ordinary rifles/mounted guns' ammunition abstract for the initial battle; their shots cost AP but have no magazine/reload cycle. Explicit resource weapons such as this carbine, jetpacks and missile racks retain their charges. A normal failed hit roll causes no secondary damage in the first resolver. Solid/friendly obstructions must already be handled by the targeting/line-of-sight rules; do not turn a miss animation into an extra hit on a hidden entity.

## 6. Second encounter and art needs

The relay acquisition below remains a follow-on scenario proposal, not the final story or an automatically included POC mission.

The relay-station job starts with a wheeled gun-armed Tin Can in a shed. Reach its service console, activate it, enter its cockpit and extract it under its own power. Its pilot and every other crew member must still be accounted for before final extraction. If it becomes irrecoverably immobilised, the gang can leave on foot; the primary acquisition fails but crew survival remains possible.

Model brief: riveted boiler-shaped body, protected inspection hatch, four visibly separate wheels, Nib-sized cockpit, two consistent arm sockets and an oversized gun with a readable muzzle arc. Show the hatch open/closed, pilot entry, intact/disabled wheel and empty cockpit. A later leg sheet needs stance, support footprint and damaged-leg states. Advanced handheld weapons need a recognisable power cell, sights and grips in both species' supported fits.

### Updated modular barrel contract

Retain a tall cylindrical scrap core with circular top/base, rolled ribs and curved patch panels. Authored options may change the barrel shell/cap, locomotion (wheels or legs), left/right arm modules (gun, claw, tool and later approved options), curved armor, optics/hatch, exhaust/utility parts, colors and markings. “Fully customizable” means a varied compatible catalogue; exact counts, costs and gameplay effects remain to be designed. Cosmetic parts must not create undeclared abilities.

Use stable module IDs and sockets with a manifest for connection transforms, bounds, component health, functions, animation, sound and compatibility. The Nib pilot remains a persistent character with their own AP; the vehicle owns locomotion/modules and movement-used state. Changing an arm or locomotion never resets either ledger. Validate the full assembled footprint, center/stance presentation, exit clearance, arm sweep and muzzle arcs. No vehicle boarding is allowed, with either wheels or legs.

For a later playable milestone, integrate its customization in the workshop, define its mission role, and add locomotion/weapon/AI/persistence/audio coverage before declaring the feature complete. Do not quietly add the narrated relay level. In this POC, present the module sheet as concept art and keep the runtime UI free of nonfunctional Tin Can controls.

Use greyboxes first. Do not claim new concept images exist until generated and reviewed. The machine should be amusing in silhouette without obscuring pilot location, exits, damaged systems or legal weapon reach.

## 7. Follow-on acceptance

- The pilot never acts twice by switching between ground and cockpit.
- A Tin Can cannot invoke Board against a truck, bike or another Tin Can, with either wheels or legs.
- Its dismounted pilot can invoke legal character boarding with remaining AP.
- Weapons stop when their module or operator is unavailable; functional modules can still act while locomotion is disabled.
- Incoming attackers obey actual hatch access and do not gain a free cockpit takeover.
- An empty carbine spends no charge/AP on a rejected shot; preview/reload never refill it.
- A damaged wheel changes the route preview, and a failed acquisition still allows crew retreat.
