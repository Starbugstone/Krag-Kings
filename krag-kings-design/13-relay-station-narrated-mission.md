# The boiler with opinions — a second mission

Draft 0.5 · 4 October 2026 · [Pack overview](00-overview-and-campaign.md)

**User clarification — 5 October 2026:** this is world/lore and tone exploration, **not the final scenario, a fixed second chapter, or an approved additional playable mission**. Use the current realization plan for POC scope.

This illustrative mission follows Rusthook and Brok's replacement recovery. New rules are in [file 11](11-campaign-events-rescue-and-retreat.md) and [file 12](12-tin-cans-and-nib-technology.md). Dialogue and outcomes are examples, not a fixed sequence or a claim that a second playable encounter exists.

## 1. News while the leg is fitted

**Design.** The gang returned on day 1. Brok's piston-leg treatment runs until day 4. Advancing time now evaluates daily events and stops for decisions. In this example, a Scavenger rumour appears on day 2: a rival occupies an industrial relay station containing a wheeled Tin Can. The lead stays on the map; this particular job has no hidden expiry.

Brok is unavailable despite having chosen Sure Grip. On day 4, treatment completes and the clock stops to report that he is ready. His new leg grants Power Step: ordinary Move 12 m and Sprint 20 m, as well as restoring the injured leg. These are provisional implant values, independent of his learned boarding perk.

**Story.**

The visitor draws the machine in spilled sand on the garage floor. It looks like a boiler with wheels and a gun much too large for the rest of it.

Gorr studies the drawing.

“Gun's good.”

From the treatment bench, Brok points at the wheels. “Needs legs.”

“You need to finish getting yours,” says Fiz.

Two days later, Brok is already standing in the truck when Gorr reaches the yard. Every time he shifts his weight, the new piston makes a solid clunk.

“It works,” Fiz tells him.

“Checking.”

“You checked the step flat.”

Gorr examines the leg and the damaged step. “Good leg.”

That settles the inspection.

## 2. The job and the crew

**Design.** Recover the Tin Can under its own power. Reach its service console, activate it, place a compatible pilot inside, clear the freight route and extract the machine. Gorr and Fiz occupy the friendly truck's operating stations; Brok and Tikk ride as passengers. There is no bike to retrieve in this encounter.

Tikk carries the proposed Needlebeam carbine with six energy charges. The enemy fixture uses a truck with driver/gunner plus one foot guard. The Tin Can begins unoccupied in the shed. Exact routes and enemy placement require greybox testing; the Nib service passage has a slower wider alternative so one blocked route does not make the encounter impossible.

**Story.**

Tikk unwraps a narrow weapon with a glassy sight and a row of tiny illuminated symbols.

Gorr frowns. “Where's the barrel?”

“There.”

“That's a hole.”

“It's enough hole.”

Fiz starts the engine before anyone can improve it.

## 3. Two approaches

**Design.** The truck uses the main lane while Tikk dismounts and takes the maintenance passage. Gorr spends his own actions aiming/firing. Tikk's weapon can make accurate standard shots or spend both actions and two charges on a focused pulse; neither mode fires through solid cover.

The following scenes span alternating activations. Movement, interactions and shots keep their ordinary AP costs.

**Story.**

Shots hammer the truck's front plate. One clips Gorr's arm. He inspects the mark and leans towards Fiz's window.

“They've got a decent one.”

“Please stop reviewing their weapons and use yours.”

At the service passage, Tikk waits for the guard to expose himself beyond a concrete edge. He aims and fires a standard pulse. The carbine gives a sharp electrical crack. Its energy display falls from six charges to five.

“That yours?” Gorr asks over the radio.

“Yes.”

“Sounds broken.”

“It hit.”

“So does a dropped spanner.”

## 4. Brok tests the upgrade

**Design.** Fiz delivers Brok close to the rival rig. Brok uses one action for a legal board and one for a reachable claw strike. Sure Grip and the claw improve the risky check; the old leg penalty is gone. Power Step helps his ordinary foot approaches, not the boarding gap or jetpack range. The first strike damages the gun mounting rather than automatically destroying it.

**Story.**

Brok lands on the running board. The new leg compresses, catches his weight and pushes him upright.

“Good leg.”

His claw closes on an exposed bracket. Metal bends, but the gun still works.

The gunner turns. “Oh, you again.”

Brok's iron jaw clicks.

Apparently introductions travel faster than trucks.

## 5. A Nib inside something unreasonable

**Design.** Tikk reaches the service panel, activates the machine and enters on separate legal actions. The cockpit fits a Nib. The chassis carries the heavy gun; its weight is not an implant burden on Tikk's body. Moving uses one pilot AP and the chassis movement allowance. Firing uses another.

**Story.**

Inside the shed, lights flicker behind the Tin Can's crooked visor. Something begins turning. Something else probably shouldn't.

Tikk climbs into the cockpit and pulls the hatch down.

The speaker wakes with a squeal. “Testing.”

Fiz winces. “Quieter.”

Tikk adjusts a dial.

“TESTING.”

Gorr beams. “Better.”

## 6. Take the road

**Design.** The enemy driver withdraws with Brok still aboard. Broken machinery obstructs the Tin Can's direct approach. It must drive around: **a Tin Can cannot board a vehicle**, regardless of wheels or future legs. Brok can contest the enemy truck because he is a character occupying its boarding nodes.

On a later activation, Brok finishes disabling the damaged gun mount. He has not captured the truck. The driver still controls it, and trying to take it would require more fighting and another available driver.

**Story.**

Tikk turns towards the gap and stops.

“Go round,” Fiz tells him.

“He's getting away.”

“Brok's on him.”

From the other radio comes a hydraulic whine and an ugly metallic crunch.

“Still here,” says Brok.

The Tin Can takes the freight lane. Once Tikk has a clear target away from Brok, he fires its gun. The burst rocks the chassis and shakes a loose panel into his lap.

Gorr stops talking.

Then: “Fiz.”

“You cannot have his arms fitted to you.”

Gorr watches the barrels slow. “One arm.”

## 7. Get the whole team out

**Design.** A hit disables one Tin Can wheel, reducing its travel. It can still leave under its own power; wheel replacement waits for the garage. Fiz stops at a recovery point, and Brok takes a legal route back to the friendly truck. No field wheel repair is assumed.

Extracting the Tin Can secures the primary objective, but final extraction waits for every friendly or an explicit decision to leave someone behind. In this run both vehicles and all four characters leave. Their one-day motorised return advances the campaign clock to day 5, processing any eligible event rather than skipping time.

**Story.**

Tikk opens the hatch at the fort. Warm dust escapes around his ears.

Gorr walks around the machine twice.

“Needs a claw.”

“Needs a wheel bearing,” Fiz says.

“After that.”

Tikk looks at the empty mounting.

For once, nobody disagrees.

## 8. Outcomes beyond the successful story

**If the Tin Can cannot move:** Tikk exits. The gang can abandon the acquisition and return in the truck. With no working transport, they may leave on foot with portable scrap; a salvage-return marker records the stranded machine without inventing towing.

**If Brok is downed:** there is no bleed-out countdown. A surviving teammate must recover him through a legal sling/vehicle route. Leaving him in enemy-controlled territory makes him a captive and creates a rescue opportunity. A rare fatal result is explicitly different and cannot be rescued.

**If the truck is lost:** scrap is assigned to actual conscious carriers. A crew member using the recovery sling has no scrap capacity. The return is longer and can encounter campaign events, so saving people and choosing valuable portable items replaces the original vehicle plan.

**Debrief example:** Brok started with 4 XP. A successful return with useful participation can bring him to 8, short of the next threshold at 10. Sure Grip remains learned; Power Step remains granted by his functional leg. Tikk piloting the captured Tin Can is still the same character, not an extra autonomous unit. The gang owns new machinery but must allocate an existing pilot and repair resources to use it again.
